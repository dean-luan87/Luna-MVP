# Cognitive Dual World Cognition Model v1

## Two worlds, one subject

| World | Cognitive system | Question | Inputs | Candidate outputs |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Surface / Reality World | Reality Cognition | What is represented in the external world and situation? | evidence, space, physical relations, capability, resource | Reality, Situation, Feasibility candidates |
| Inner / Meaning World | Emotional Cognition | What may this represented world mean to this subject? | relationship, identity, experience, value, emotional context | meaning, relational, value, emotional candidates |

Example: a person three metres away is a Reality representation. “This person
is a friend and their support may strengthen our relationship” is a meaning
candidate, not a physical fact.

## Coupling and isolation

The worlds may share Identity and Experience references. They must remain
isolated in inference criteria, purposes, and authority:

- Reality Cognition does not assign relationship meaning or emotion.
- Emotional Cognition cannot establish Reality truth, erase uncertainty, or
  mutate World State.
- Neither world creates a Decision, Action, State mutation, or provider call.

This v1 extends—not replaces—the already frozen Reality / Emotional Cognition
boundary.
