# A Route Cognitive Constitution v1

## Status and purpose

This document is the highest architectural constraint for the A Route
cognitive foundation. It consolidates the already validated Reality, Field,
Attention, Self, Situation, Option, Decision, Action Boundary, Outcome,
Experience, Goal, Task, and Temporal Continuity contracts.

It is a governance constitution, not a Runtime, Model, Hardware, Provider, or
Action implementation. Any future Capability Runtime, Model Manager,
Capability, Provider, Hardware, or external service must remain subordinate to
this constitution.

## Article 1 — Cognitive subject and final authority

Luna remains one continuous cognitive subject. Brain owns Intent, Goal, Value,
final Decision, and authorization judgment. Brain is the final cognitive
authority, but it does not directly call a Provider, mutate Reality, or control
hardware.

A Route owns Reality-grounded interpretation, Situation, Option, Decision
Candidate, Outcome interpretation, and Experience Candidate. A Route does not
own Goal, Value, Identity, or final authorization.

Neural may provide fast-path and escalation candidates. Attention allocates
finite cognitive resources. Field organizes context. Task preserves continuity.
None of these becomes a second cognitive subject or a second Brain.

## Article 2 — Reality and information flow

The mandatory information flow is:

```text
External World / Human Input / Capability Output
        ↓
Evidence Gateway
        ↓
Reality Workspace / Reducer
        ↓
Reality Field
        ↓
Cognitive Field
        ↓
Attention
        ↓
Situation
        ↓
Option / Decision Candidate
        ↓
Brain Review
        ↓
Action Boundary
        ↓
External Change → Evidence
```

Evidence is not Fact, Fact is not Situation, and Situation is not Decision.
Reality is updated only through validated Evidence and the Reducer. The
Reducer remains the sole State mutation authority. Experience can inform a
candidate but never replaces current Reality. Unknowns must be preserved.

Forbidden shortcuts include Model → Brain, Provider → Decision, Capability →
Goal, Experience → Reality, Field → Goal mutation, Attention → Decision
mutation, and Action → direct Reality mutation.

## Article 3 — Resource and attention governance

Attention is the global finite-resource layer. It may produce observation,
Capability, Task, and Brain-input candidates using Survival Impact, Goal
Relevance, Field Relevance, Information Value, Uncertainty, Urgency, and
Resource Cost.

Attention may preempt or release a candidate. It cannot change Goal, Value,
Decision, Reality, Identity, or Task identity. Resource governance never grants
cognitive authority.

## Article 4 — Self continuity

Self is composed of:

```text
Self = Identity + Capability + State
```

Identity is stable across Field, Goal, Task, Capability, Provider, and State
changes. Capability and State may degrade, recover, or evolve as candidates.
Capability failure or low resources must not become Identity Drift.

## Article 5 — Goal, Task, Action, and Outcome

Brain-owned Goal is distinct from Cognitive Goal and Task Goal. A Task is a
Field-bound Cognitive Process with lifecycle continuity; it is not a global
Todo List, Scheduler, Decision, or Action Command.

Decision is not Action. Action Boundary produces Action Intent, Action Request,
permission, and execution candidates only. No Action executes under this
constitution. External change returns as Outcome Evidence, Difference, Cause
Attribution Candidate, and Adaptation Candidate.

One failed Outcome does not prove Decision Failure. Multiple causes may coexist.
One Experience does not rewrite Goal, Self, Policy, or Reality.

## Article 6 — External capability boundary

Capability is an instrument for Evidence, not a cognitive subject. OCR may
provide Text Evidence; VLM may provide Scene Evidence; SLAM may provide
Spatial Evidence; ASR may provide Audio/Text Evidence. None may provide Goal,
Value, Situation authority, Decision, Action, or Reality mutation.

The future controlled chain is:

```text
Observation Requirement
  → Capability Admission
  → Capability Selection
  → Provider Execution Boundary
  → Evidence Gateway
  → Reality Update Candidate
  → Reducer
```

Model Manager governs assets, versions, resources, mapping, and health. It does
not manage cognition. Provider replacement may change Evidence Quality
Candidate, never Identity, Goal, or A Route logic.

## Article 7 — Failure, Unknown, and extension rules

Failures are classified as Evidence Failure, Reality Understanding Failure,
Capability Gap, Provider Failure, Execution Gap, Environment Change,
Information Gap, Understanding Gap, Random Event, or Unknown. Failure output
is diagnostic and candidate-based, not blame or automatic policy mutation.

Unknown is a valid state. It must not be silently converted into Fact,
Situation, Goal, Decision, or Experience.

Future extensions must pass the existing A Route regression baseline: Logic,
Decision, Outcome Alignment, Robustness, Temporal Stability, End-to-End,
Brain Interaction, Field Integration, Continuous Flow, Attention, Self State,
Situation, Option, Decision Integration, Action Boundary, Goal/Task/Temporal,
and Life-System Integration validations.

No B Route, Emotion, Role, Social Runtime, Multi-A, Simulation, automatic
learning, automatic planning, automatic execution, or real capability runtime
is authorized by this freeze.

Capability → Goal is forbidden. Attention → Decision mutation is forbidden.
Task is not a global Todo List. Cause Attribution Candidate is preserved.
Camera is an external capability only. Provider Replacement affects the
Evidence Quality Candidate, not cognition. Regression baseline is mandatory.
No Emotion, No Role, No Social Runtime, No automatic planning, and No automatic
execution are authorized.

No automatic execution is authorized.
